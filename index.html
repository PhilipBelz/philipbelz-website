A reflection on process coaching

How coaching changed the way I lead

My time at the College of Executive Coaching changed how I think about developing leaders. I began bringing the listening and questioning skills I learned there into my process improvement work. That blend became what I call process coaching.

I use it to help early-career professionals and emerging leaders work through real challenges with their teams. We might clarify who owns a step, improve a handoff, or agree on how to measure progress. I want them to leave with skills they can use the next time a problem comes up.

Process Improvement Engagement

Work through one business problem, from understanding what it costs you to deciding how to fix it.

  • A shared definition of the problem. A one-page project brief with the workflow’s boundaries, owner, desired outcome, and baseline measures.
  • A business case and current-state map. See the handoffs, delays, errors, and rework, with the costs and assumptions behind the opportunity.
  • Solution options and a redesigned process. Compare process changes, automation, and AI by impact, effort, and risk. Define responsibilities, review points, and exception handling.
  • A 30-, 60-, and 90-day action plan. Set owners, measures, dependencies, and review dates for immediate fixes, testing, and decisions about adoption.
How we work together

We begin with a kickoff and short staff interviews using real examples. In a working session, we walk a typical case and an exception through the proposed redesign. You receive the maps, business case, and action plan in editable form, followed by a readout to confirm decisions and next steps.

The starting scope covers one workflow. We confirm the fixed fee and schedule before beginning, based on the teams, handoffs, and evidence involved.

AI Strategy Foundation

Set your first AI strategy around the work your business needs to improve.

  • A strategy grounded in your business. Agree on objectives, decision owners, and scope. Assess workflows, existing AI use, tools, data, and staff readiness across up to four business functions at the starting price.
  • A prioritized opportunity portfolio. Short business cases set out expected benefits, implementation effort, costs, risks, and assumptions. Rank opportunities by value and effort, and decide what to defer.
  • A readiness plan for the first pilot. Map the proposed workflow with your IT lead and identify governance, policy, architecture, and training needs, with owners for unresolved questions.
  • A written strategy and phased roadmap. Know what happens first, who owns it, which dependencies need attention, and when to review results before further investment.
How we work together

We start with a leadership kickoff and interviews across the agreed functions. A prioritization session brings business owners together to select the first opportunity. A technical walkthrough with your IT lead explores data flow, permissions, human review, and fallback needs.

You receive the written strategy, ranked portfolio, and roadmap before a closeout session to confirm decisions and owners. We agree on the interview count and schedule during scoping.

How I approach a problem

Find the friction
